# Nanobody R&D campaign — 2026-09-09

## Decision

Keep mining and checkpoint publication disabled. The campaign recovered the
competitive P20809 binding pose, but it did not produce a sequence that is
competitive across the complete ten-metric objective. The public teacher and
its close variants have good interface geometry and poor peptide-liability
ranks. Attempts to repair that framework either fail the current IgBLAST
humanness gate or preserve the original liability score.

The next campaign should start from a current-validator-compliant,
low-liability VHH scaffold and co-design its CDR geometry and sequence against
the learned epitope. It should not continue local optimization of the present
teacher family.

The winners are not 40 unrelated solutions. They are a single converged
structural family. Most residues are invariant; recurring variation is
concentrated at raw sequence positions 1–3, 29, 31, 62, 73, 84, 108, 110,
111, 116, and 118–127. Much of that variation is terminal or framework-side,
while the binding core is strongly conserved. Those substitutions are useful
as an empirical proposal distribution, but their epoch frequency alone does
not establish causality because every score is a noisy single structure
prediction and the ten metrics trade against one another.

The exceptional recent winner was epoch 25009 with rank sum 59.0, design
iIPTM 0.79705, and liability 235/27. The latest epoch 25022 winner scored
106.5 with design iIPTM 0.78231, target iPTM 0.69236, interaction PAE
10.63017, four hydrogen bonds, three salt bridges, buried SASA 712.60, and
liability 260/29. Thus a score near 50 is excellent rather than typical. The
submitted result near 167 was still clearly outside the competitive band.

## Corrected epitope and structural teacher

Refolding strong public designs exposed a configuration error: the original
configured hotspot was not the interface used by competitive structures. The
consensus target-contact positions, using canonical target residue indices,
are:

```text
11, 14–15, 17–18, 21–22, 25–26, 29, 94–95, 98–99, 101–102, 105, 109
```

The public structural teacher reproduced the reference geometry: design iIPTM
0.77849, design pTM 0.88672, target iPTM 0.6865, minimum target PAE 2.3595,
interaction PAE 10.67815, eight hydrogen bonds, two salt bridges, and buried
SASA 735.71. It is valuable as a pose teacher, not as a sequence to submit.

Small terminal variants retained or slightly improved the pose. Repeated
exact scoring put Q3K at mean iIPTM 0.78449 and L4M at 0.79119, but both fail
the live 0.85 human-framework threshold. Five current-valid variants passed
the complete Layer 2 and novelty replay; their best selection rank was 188.5
(P127I), and their liability remained 265/30. Local sequence search therefore
did not solve the limiting objective.

## Framework-repair experiments

All liability measurements below use the exact BoltzGen peptide-liability
implementation. All current-valid claims use the live IgBLAST thresholds,
not archived acceptance status.

| Experiment | Scale | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| Fixed-backbone inverse folding, ordinary sampling | 494 unique | Best liability 230/27, but the best designs had at least 15 mutations. |
| Fixed-backbone inverse folding, low temperature | 148 unique | Best liability again 230/27. |
| Fixed-backbone inverse folding, liability-prone residues banned | 300 unique | Liability reached 100/13; Layer 2 rejected all top 30: 24 low human identity and 6 non-VHH. |

The four Layer-2 survivors were Q3S+L55V, Q3S+L55I, Q3T+L55V, and Q3N+L55V.
They all had human-framework score 0.854293, nativeness 0.692430, and liability
265/30. This directly tests the proposal to borrow chemically conservative
amino acids from known VHHs: the strategy is reasonable as a constrained
proposal operator, but on this teacher its useful moves lie across the live
humanness boundary. It did not provide a competitive repair path.

Exact target-complex scoring closed the remaining uncertainty. Across the four
survivors, design iIPTM ranged from 0.68832 to 0.77047, target iPTM from
0.59157 to 0.67569, interaction PAE from 10.88791 to 12.64902, and buried SASA
from 664.91 to 697.53. All four kept liability 265/30. The teacher itself was
better on the central confidence and interface-area measures (iIPTM 0.77849,
target iPTM 0.6865, interaction PAE 10.67815, buried SASA 735.71). Q3S+L55V
increased hydrogen bonds from eight to nine and salt bridges from two to three,
but lost confidence and interface area. No survivor is a Pareto improvement.

## Why naive framework transfer fails

VHH framework residues help determine CDR3 conformation; "similar chemical
property" is not enough to guarantee that a substitution is structurally
neutral. Controlled CDR-grafting work found that grafting onto a humanized
framework could reduce both affinity and thermal stability, and that specific
framework backmutations restored them. Other VHH structure studies identify
FR2 and residues around CDR3 as part of the supporting geometry. This is why
the experiments protected CDRs, antigen contacts, Kabat Vernier positions,
and VHH hallmark positions and then required an independent complex refold.

The negative result is more informative than a larger blind sweep: the strong
teacher is too close to the current humanness threshold to permit enough
framework movement to repair liability.

## Next experimental architecture

1. Select several scaffolds that already pass the *current* IgBLAST/TNP gates
   with margin and have low exact liability. Archived acceptance is not enough.
2. Co-design CDR backbone, sequence, and pose on the corrected epitope. Run a
   small factorial screen across scaffold, hotspot subset, CDR3 length, target
   conformation, and generator instead of expanding one local mutation tree.
3. Use BoltzGen plus an independent proposal lane such as RFantibody, IgGM, or
   mBER. Use AntiFold/EasyNano only after a competitive pose exists; they are
   refiners, not reliable wrong-dock rescue mechanisms.
4. Apply current IgBLAST-only filtering immediately after sequence generation,
   before CPU-heavy TNP. Treat Llamanade, Sapiens, and AbNatiV2 as proposal
   priors, while the live validator remains authoritative.
5. Preserve all ten objectives and Pareto-filter candidates. Do not collapse
   pre-selection to one sequence proxy until held-parent experiments show
   top-tail recall under exact scoring.
6. Exact-score in small, continuously mergeable batches and repeat-score the
   leading Pareto candidates. This permits checkpoint improvements during an
   epoch without waiting for one monolithic batch.

The promotion criterion should be a novel, current-valid sequence whose
repeated ten-metric rank is competitive with reconstructed recent winners.
Until then, the producer should not publish a checkpoint.

## Reproducibility and operations

- Candidate generation, IgBLAST/TNP, liability scoring, archive analysis, and
  exact scoring are exposed as locked Pixi tasks.
- Ansible owns the Pixi activation environment, CUDA compiler/include paths,
  IgBLAST database path, TNP import shim, and the ImmuneBuilder/OpenMM runtime
  compatibility patch.
- The latest clean deployment completed 46 Ansible tasks and all artifact
  canaries in 2 minutes 7 seconds. Warm Pixi environment checks take roughly
  1.5–2 seconds each; most remaining repeat-deploy time is Ansible remote task
  setup, directory iteration, source synchronization, and canaries.
- No miner or checkpoint publisher was run during this campaign.
